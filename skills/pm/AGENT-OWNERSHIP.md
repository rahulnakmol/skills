# Agent ownership

The PM who manages an agent fleet is not just a user of agents — they are an owner, responsible in both directions: for what the fleet catches that the PM would have missed, and for what the PM catches that the fleet would have missed. Neither direction is optional, and neither is the default outcome of simply running more agents.

## Covering your blind spots

Ask the agent fleet for the lens you are missing, not the one that agrees with you. A perspective-diverse panel of critics — one reading for market risk, one for organizational resistance, one for technical feasibility — surfaces disagreement a single reviewer would not produce. Provocations from the grill's intuition agents push past the safe, familiar framing a PM defaults to under time pressure. Deep research fills the evidence gap a PM does not have time to close alone. Used this way, the fleet's job is to disagree productively, not to confirm what the PM already believed walking in.

## Assessing the agents' blind spots

Before any gate, the PM runs a short pre-gate checklist against whatever the fleet produced:

- **Coverage and omissions** — what did the brief, the case, or the review simply not address? A clean-looking document can still leave coverage gaps unaddressed.
- **Unevidenced claims** — every strong claim should trace to an entry in the evidence corpus; a claim with no citation is a guess wearing evidence's clothing.
- **Unrunnable acceptance** — could each claim the artifact makes be restated as a gate with a check and an expected result (`grit/LEDGER.md`)? A claim no check could ever test is not evidence-bearing, whatever its citation.
- **Ambition collapsed to star 5** — agents tuned to avoid controversy tend to settle on the safe middle score; a review that lands everything near star 5 has probably not actually stretched for star 7 or star 8.
- **Correlated agreement** — reviewers built on the same underlying model produce correlated verdicts, not independent ones; three of them agreeing is one opinion said three times.
- **The do-nothing test** — did the analysis seriously consider not acting at all, or did it treat action as a foregone conclusion?

## Growing the owner

Per the repository's own growth principle, progress is measured by the PM running this checklist unprompted — not because a skill told them to this time, but because it has become how they read agent output by default. An owner who still needs the checklist spelled out every gate has not yet grown into the role; an owner who catches an omission the checklist did not even name has grown past it.
