# The leadership report method

A status update that leads with activity is not a report, it is a diary. This is the method `report` follows to produce something a leader can act on in the time they actually have.

## The leadership pack

The leadership pack is the single artifact `report` produces at every cadence: a short, visual, decision-oriented brief, never a raw export of the registers. It carries its compressing figure per `VISUALS.md` — a leadership pack without one is text pretending to be a briefing — and it states the decisions the initiative needs from leadership, not just the decisions already made.

## Value first

The pack opens with value, not activity: what benefit moved, what north star contribution was made, sourced from `realize`'s register. Only after value is stated does the pack move to what was worked on. A reader who stops after the first section should already know whether the initiative is worth their continued attention.

## The 4Ps

Every pack is structured as four sections. Progress — what moved since the last cadence, sourced from the delivery pipeline's live pickup-protocol state, pull request activity, and stacks. Problems — the risks and issues actively blocking or threatening the initiative, sourced from `raid`. Priorities — what happens next and why it is next, sourced from `roadmap`'s sequencing. Perspective — the PM's own insight and judgment call for this cadence; an agent may draft it, but the PM owns what it says, because a leadership pack with no human perspective in it is just a dashboard with a cover page.

## Sources

The pack is assembled from the registers and pipeline state, never hand-typed from memory: `realize` for value and north star rollup, `raid` for Problems, `roadmap` for Priorities, `case` for the cost and benefit baseline the pack measures against, and the live tracker for pull request and pickup-protocol status. Rendering to a polished document goes through `Call the Skill tool with "press"`.

## Cadences

`report` runs at five cadences, each at its own altitude: weekly (team-level Progress and Problems), fortnightly (the same, aggregated), quarterly (Priorities and north-star trend added), half-yearly (portfolio-level Perspective), and yearly (the full transformation-value story). A weekly pack that tries to carry yearly altitude buries the reader in noise; a yearly pack that stays at weekly altitude buries the decision.
