# The case method

A case is the artifact the sponsor approves at the Investment gate — it exists to make a spending decision defensible, not to justify a decision already made informally elsewhere.

## Options considered

A case names at least two real options for solving the problem, never a single path presented as inevitable. Each option is described in enough detail that a sponsor could choose either one and know roughly what they were signing up for — cost shape, timeline shape, and the risk each option carries.

## The do-nothing option

Every case includes a third option: do nothing. What does inaction cost, in the same terms the other options are costed in? A case that never seriously considers not acting treats action as a foregone conclusion, and a sponsor who is never shown the cost of inaction cannot actually weigh the decision they are being asked to make.

## Costs and benefits

Each option carries its costs and its benefits in the same units — see `COSTING.md` for the full build, run, opportunity, and agent-fleet accounting — and its benefits trace forward to the register `realize` will later hold accountable for delivering them. A benefit that cannot be measured later should not be claimed now.

## Sensitivity

The case names which single assumption, if wrong, kills the recommended option — the sensitivity that actually matters to the sponsor's decision, not a mechanical list of every variable that could move. A case whose recommendation survives no matter which assumption is challenged has probably not been pressure-tested; a case grilled before the Investment gate should be able to name its own weakest point without being asked twice.
