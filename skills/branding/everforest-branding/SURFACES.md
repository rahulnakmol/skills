# Everforest surface direction

## Composition and imagery

Use an orderly grid, generous margins, fine rules, and blocks that feel placed rather than floated. Pair close views of natural material with wider images that show people using a space or product. Avoid generic leaf overlays and green color casts.

## Product, documents, and data

- Use the blue-green `focus` role for keyboard focus and selected controls. Keep green `accent` for orientation, charts, and short emphasis.
- Use solid boundaries and labels for state. Do not rely on several neighboring greens to distinguish data.
- Use the light mode for print and long documents. Uncoated paper supports the theme, but a proof decides whether low-saturation surfaces remain distinct.
- Use Fraunces for one clear argument per slide, not for paragraphs or chart labels.

## Environmental and physical work

Start with warm off-white walls, charcoal wayfinding, and green accents on limited planes, furniture, or graphics. Specify matte and low-sheen samples before broad wall application. Measure Lab and light reflectance values on the actual substrate under day and artificial light. Wood and plant material can support the direction, but they do not replace contrast and sign-legibility tests.

## Motion and video

Use calm camera movement, stable horizons, natural depth, and transitions based on cuts, occlusion, or gentle dissolves. Keep skin, white objects, and safety colors neutral. Build warmth through lighting and production design before the grade. Use the theme greens in titles, charts, and controlled set details rather than applying a green wash to footage. A reduced-motion cut removes simulated camera movement while preserving the message order.
