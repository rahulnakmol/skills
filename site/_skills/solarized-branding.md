## What it does

Solarized Branding turns a symmetric screen palette into a restrained cross-surface identity. IBM Plex Serif, Sans, and Mono supply open-source display, reading, and technical roles. The theme defines accessible light and dark semantic tokens. The common engine supplies verbal identity, surface production, accessibility, physical sampling, video, provenance, and delivery contracts.

## A working example

QuenServe E1 uses Solarized light for the product's reading view, release PDF, and event-wall sampling brief. Dark mode supports the video titles and an evening display. Blue remains the interaction and principal-series color. Gold identifies warning only with a label and shape.

The wall uses measured warm neutral samples because ambient light can shift them sharply. The video balances footage before selective blue-green and gold shaping. Skin, white objects, source screens, safety colors, and chart evidence stay protected through Rec.709 and Display P3 transforms.

## Common questions

<details class="qa"><summary>Are light and dark automatic inversions?</summary><div class="qa-body"><p>No. They share a tonal model, but each role and rendered state is tested separately against its actual adjacent colors.</p></div></details>

<details class="qa"><summary>Should every source accent appear?</summary><div class="qa-body"><p>No. One main accent usually carries identity. Other colors remain bounded to labeled status or data roles.</p></div></details>

<details class="qa"><summary>Can warm screen values specify a wall?</summary><div class="qa-body"><p>No. The wall requires measured samples, recorded lighting, substrate and finish, glare and legibility review, and owner approval.</p></div></details>

## It's working if

- Light and dark modes preserve the same meaning while passing their own checks.
- The Plex serif, sans, and mono roles remain distinct and readable.
- Blue and gold retain bounded interaction, identity, data, or status jobs.
- Final video files retain neutral evidence, readable captions, and correct color tags.

## Where it fits
