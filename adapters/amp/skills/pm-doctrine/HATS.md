# The two hats

A PM in this practice carries two identities, never blended: the product hat and the transformation hat. Each has its own artifacts, its own persona style, and its own relationship to the TOM — but both pass through the same four gates, and both draw on the same skill fleet.

## One hat at a time

An initiative is framed under exactly one hat. Mixing them mid-initiative produces documents that answer to neither audience well — a PRD written half for end-user personas and half for organizational RACI satisfies neither reviewer. `discover` classifies the hat at intake from the shape of the problem statement, and `ask-pm` asks the PM directly only when the classification is genuinely ambiguous — never as a routine first question.

## The product hat

Under the product hat, the TOM is optional — most product work does not require a full target operating model. Personas are end users: named, with behaviors and feelings, the kind of persona a PRD's acceptance criteria are written against. Success is measured with product-led-growth metrics — activation, retention, expansion — and delivery often rolls out behind feature flags so exposure can be controlled and measured before a full launch.

## The transformation hat

Under the transformation hat, the TOM is mandatory: every initiative runs through `tom-architect`'s process decomposition and maturity assessment before an epic manifest is drafted. Personas are organizational actors — a claims processor, a regional finance lead — described with a RACI role rather than a feeling. Epics originate from maturity gaps the TOM surfaces or from statement-of-work milestones already committed to a sponsor, and rollout proceeds through milestone gates rather than flag-controlled exposure.

## Operational and design work

Both hats span the same range of work from a business and functional standpoint — discovery interviews, process mapping, backlog shaping, sponsor reporting — and the four gates apply identically under either. What changes between the hats is not the rigor of the work, only the audience the artifacts are written for and whether the TOM sits in the critical path.
